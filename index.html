LoCoFusion

Is someone standing in the doorway? A low-cost camera and a 24 GHz mmWave radar, fused on an ESP32-S3 to answer that more reliably than either sensor alone.

Read the overview View on GitHub

UCLA EC ENGR M202A (Embedded Systems), Fall 2026

Two cheap sensors, different blind spots

Lighting, HVAC, and security all need to know if someone is present. The doorway is the natural place to ask, but each cheap sensor fails in its own way.

Camera

Knows what a person looks like

Few false alarms from objects, but fails in the dark and when the view is partly blocked.

mmWave radar

Senses presence in the dark

Sees through light obstructions and even breathing, but can't tell a person from a fan, curtain, or pet.

Their failure modes barely overlap, so fusing them should beat either one alone. Both run on a single ESP32-S3 that outputs one answer: PRESENT or ABSENT. No counting, identifying, or tracking.

Research question

Can a lightweight ML model that combines camera and radar detect presence more reliably than either sensor alone, within the limits of an ESP32-S3?

Reliability

How much does fusion improve accuracy, precision, and recall?

Learned vs. rule

Does a small trained classifier beat a hand-written rule?

Robustness

Does it degrade gracefully when one sensor is impaired or fails?

Cost

What does each approach cost in latency, memory, and update rate?

How it works

Each sensor has its own pipeline. Fusion combines only their high-level outputs, so the model stays small enough for a microcontroller.

Board

ESP32-S3

Freenove WROOM CAM with onboard camera. 240 MHz dual-core, 16 MB flash, 8 MB PSRAM.

Radar

24 GHz

HLK-LD2410C mmWave sensor over UART. Reports target state, distance, and signal energy.

Logging

microSD

Timestamped, synchronized sensor readings and ground-truth labels.

Four approaches, compared

Camera-only

Baseline. Present if person confidence clears a threshold.

Radar-only

Baseline. Present if a target is within the doorway's range.

Rule-based fusion

Trust the camera when confident, fall back to radar in the dark.

ML fusion

Logistic regression or a tiny neural net trained on our data.

Evaluation

Six conditions, each chosen to stress one sensor's weakness. Detection quality and on-device cost are measured side by side.

  • Normal lightingBoth sensors working well
  • DarknessCamera degrades; radar carries the decision
  • Partial obstructionPerson partly hidden; camera confidence drops
  • Sensor failureOne sensor disconnected or zeroed
  • Empty doorwayFalse-positive rate with no one present
  • Moving objectsFans and swinging doors the camera should reject

Metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 per condition, alongside latency, decision rate, and flash and RAM use.
